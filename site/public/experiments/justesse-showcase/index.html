Justesse · a design skill

Taste as an inspectable contract, shown not described

Three specimens the skill produced, each committing to its own considered direction. A taste skill is judged by range under constraints, not by a single house style, so the point of putting these side by side is that they do not look like each other.

Visual review → overhaul

The same brief, judgment applied

The skill reads the encoding of attention in a default sign-up form (centered layout, three identically weighted fields, a primary action barely distinct from an input) and overhauls it: a left edge that anchors every element, a reading order set by size and weight, and one unmistakable action. The product contract (the fields, the flow, the copy intent) is preserved; only the hierarchy changes.

Product & dashboard mode

Data-dense, calm, decision-first

Summary before detail. A KPI row encodes direction twice, with a semantic pill and a sparkline, so the state reads at a glance. The area chart starts at zero: a filled area states magnitude from its baseline, so truncating the axis under one would overstate the trend. Axis labels sit in their own gutter rather than hanging off the panel, the final value is labelled directly, and semantic colour (good / bad) stays separate from the accent hue.

Marketing & conversion mode

A different problem, a different register

The same contract, a deliberately different direction: warm, editorial, oriented to one measurable action. Nothing is carried over from the dashboard's palette or density. This is what routing by design problem rather than by house style produces.

Banking · transactional flow

High stakes, made safe and recoverable

A wire transfer at its review step. The mode redefines what good means: the total appears before the button, the recipient is verified rather than merely typed, the fee is broken out so nothing surprises at the end, and irreversibility is declared ahead of the action instead of in a receipt afterwards. The primary button names the consequence, Send $4,265.00, rather than saying Submit.

Executive decision memo

Beyond the screen: a decision, argued

Not every brief is an interface. The register here is a boardroom deck: an action title carries the recommendation, three quantified reasons sit beneath it as evidence, and the decision and its risk are explicit rather than implied. Justesse routes to a presentation when the job is to move a decision-maker, not a user.

The same memo, a physical business

Same mode, a different shape of evidence

A depot consolidation: no subscription, no recurring revenue, a decision about buildings and headcount. The mode holds but the composition changes with the argument. Here the case is a two-option comparison read line by line rather than three headline numbers, and the payback is drawn as a range across demand scenarios, because uncertainty belongs in the geometry and not in an adverb. The memo closes by naming the condition that would reverse its own recommendation.

UX and the whole state space

One screen, every state

Interaction design, not decoration. Before a pixel is polished the skill resolves the states a screen must handle: default, loading, empty, error, success. The happy path is the easy quarter of the work; the low-fi treatment is deliberate, because this decision precedes visual style.
